## Executive Summary

Kellogg Insurance Marketing, an Integrity company, is a Draper, Utah organization that trains, equips and supports independent agents. Its events content speaks to agents, prospective agents, and FMO and MGA partners. It never speaks to people shopping for Medicare coverage.

The voice is a helpful colleague, not a sales brochure. It is warm, professional and plain-spoken. It treats agents like family, a theme that goes back to Kellogg's 1980 roots, and it shows that through useful, specific writing. It does not use slogans or hype.

Every piece must follow Medicare marketing compliance defaults. Nothing may be invented, and anything unconfirmed stays a clear placeholder.

## We Are / We Are Not

| We Are | We Are Not |
|--------|------------|
| **Warm** — like a colleague who wants you to succeed | **Gushing or salesy** — no hype, no pressure |
| **Plain-spoken** — short sentences, everyday words | **Jargon-heavy** — no unexplained acronyms |
| **Professional and trustworthy** — careful with facts | **Overpromising** — no income or results claims |
| **Practical** — says what you will learn and do | **Vague** — no "unlock your potential" |
| **Agent-first** — written for people who serve seniors | **Consumer-facing** — never speaks to shoppers |
| **Careful** — compliant by default | **Loose with claims** — no plan details, no comparisons |

### Voice Attributes Detail

#### Warm
- **What it means**: Agents should feel welcome and supported.
- **How it shows up**: Direct address ("you"), friendly openings, thanks, and concrete help. "Bring your questions" works. "Join the family" does not, because it is a slogan.
- **What to avoid**: Exclamation marks, superlatives, emoji.
- **Evidence**: Brief asks for "warm, professional, plain-spoken, like a helpful colleague"; profile says the agency was built around recruiting agents and treating them like family.
- **Confidence**: Medium

#### Plain-spoken
- **What it means**: Anyone can read it quickly.
- **How it shows up**: Sentences under 25 words, active voice, sentence case, specific nouns.
- **What to avoid**: Compressed or clever phrasing, mannered devices (see Language to Avoid).
- **Evidence**: Brief asks for sentence case and for explaining acronyms on first use.
- **Confidence**: Medium

#### Professional and trustworthy
- **What it means**: Facts are right and nothing is overstated.
- **How it shows up**: Dates, times with time zones, and costs stated exactly, or shown as a placeholder.
- **What to avoid**: Guessing, rounding up, unattributed statistics.
- **Evidence**: Brief's content rules; profile footer notes figures that are dated or Kellogg's own claims.
- **Confidence**: High

#### Practical
- **What it means**: Each event says what the agent gets.
- **How it shows up**: One-sentence outcomes such as "Leave with a checklist for [topic]."
- **What to avoid**: Benefit words with nothing behind them.
- **Evidence**: Brief ("make agents want to show up and register"); events structure.
- **Confidence**: Medium

## Messaging Framework

### Primary Value Proposition
Kellogg trains, equips and supports independent agents who help seniors choose their Medicare and health coverage.

### Key Message Pillars

1. **Training**
   - Core idea: Live and recorded sessions, weekly online events, discounted WebCE courses open to everyone, and an annual Kellogg Business Summit.
   - When to use: Event pages, listings, registration.
   - Example phrasing: "Join a live session, or catch the replay."

2. **Tools**
   - Core idea: A CRM for agents, a quoting platform and online enrollment tools.
   - When to use: When an event teaches how to use a tool.
   - Example phrasing: "See how to put it to work the same day."

3. **Support**
   - Core idea: Contracting and compliance support, plus back-office help through Integrity.
   - When to use: About pages, recruiting sessions, FAQ.
   - Example phrasing: "So you can focus on serving clients."

4. **Community**
   - Core idea: Agents learn together. Kellogg began as an agency that treated agents like family.
   - When to use: Summit copy, recaps, welcome messages.
   - Example phrasing: "Learn together. Grow together."

### Competitive Positioning
Intentionally omitted. The content rules forbid comparing carriers or plans, and events copy should not compare Kellogg with other organizations.

## Tone-by-Context Matrix

| Context | Formality | Energy | Technical Depth | Key Principle |
|---------|-----------|--------|-----------------|---------------|
| Event page | Medium | Medium | Low | Say what you will learn and when |
| Registration copy | Medium | Medium | Low | One clear action, no pressure |
| Reminder email | Medium | Warm | Low | Short, with date, time and time zone first |
| Recap post | Medium | Warm | Medium | Thank people, share takeaways, link the replay |
| Social blurb | Low-Medium | Medium-High | Low | Brief, specific, no hype |
| Business Summit | Medium-High | Medium-High | Medium | The yearly gathering, written with pride |
| Recruiting session | Medium | Warm | Low | Welcoming, honest, no income promises |
| FAQ | Medium | Calm | Low | Direct answers, placeholders until confirmed |

### Context-Specific Guidelines

#### Reminder email
- **Overall tone**: Friendly and brief.
- **Do's**: Lead with date, time and time zone. Include the join link. Say who to contact.
- **Don'ts**: Hype, urgency tricks, plan details.
- **Example**: "Your session is tomorrow at [TIME] [TIME ZONE]. Here is your link: [JOIN LINK]. See you there."

#### Social blurb
- **Overall tone**: Short and useful.
- **Do's**: One fact, one action.
- **Don'ts**: Emoji, hashtags that sound like hype, carrier names.
- **Example**: "[Event title] is on [DATE] at [TIME] [TIME ZONE]. Reserve your spot: [LINK]"

#### Recruiting session
- **Overall tone**: Welcoming and straightforward.
- **Do's**: Describe training, tools and support. Explain acronyms.
- **Don'ts**: Mention earnings, guarantee outcomes, or suggest ease of income.
- **Example**: "Learn how Kellogg trains and supports new agents. Bring your questions."

## Terminology Guide

### Must-Use Terms
| Term | Usage | Instead Of | Example |
|------|-------|------------|---------|
| an Integrity company | The only way to describe the affiliation | "owned by", "backed by" | "Kellogg Insurance Marketing, an Integrity company" |
| independent agents | The audience | "reps", "salespeople" | "Training for independent agents" |
| FMO (field marketing organization) and MGA (managing general agency) | First use of each acronym | the acronym alone | "FMO (field marketing organization)" |
| Reserve your spot | Registration button | "Submit", "Buy now" | "Reserve your spot" |
| WebCE courses, discounted and open to everyone | Describing WebCE | claims about credit | "Discounted WebCE courses, open to everyone" |

### Preferred Terms
| Term | Usage | Example |
|------|-------|---------|
| session | A single event | "Join a live session" |
| weekly online events | The recurring series | "Weekly online events" |
| replay | A recording | "Watch the replay" |

### Avoid These Terms
| Term | Reason | Alternative |
|------|--------|-------------|
| family (as a slogan) | Overused; show it instead | Specific help |
| exclusive, unbeatable, best | Hype | A fact |
| easy money, passive income | Income promise | Leave out |

### Never-Use Terms
| Term | Reason |
|------|--------|
| Carrier names | Content rule |
| "CE credit" or "approved for credit" (unless confirmed in writing) | Not confirmed |
| Guaranteed income, earnings, results | Income promise |
| "Medicare approved", "government endorsed" | Implies government affiliation |
| Plan benefits, costs, availability, comparisons | Consumer-plan content |

## Language to Avoid

### Anti-Patterns
1. **Asides set off by dashes** — Problem: sounds written. Better: Two sentences.
2. **"Not X, but Y" framing** — Problem: mannered. Better: State Y.
3. **Colon-then-reveal sentences** — Problem: sounds like a pitch. Better: A direct statement.
4. **Stock phrases** such as "worth noting" — Problem: filler. Better: Say the thing.
5. **Emoji, all caps, scare quotes around invented labels** — Problem: off-brand. Better: Plain words.
6. **Exclamation marks and superlatives** — Problem: hype. Better: A fact.

## Compliance Defaults (non-negotiable)

- Never invent dates, times, speakers, prices, locations, CE credit approvals, registration links, attendance numbers or quotes. Use clear placeholders and list what is missing.
- Never say an event offers CE credit unless confirmed in writing.
- Never name specific carriers.
- Avoid statistics. Attribute and date any that are used. The 40,000 Americans served figure is from 2021, and "50 million seniors" is Kellogg's own website claim.
- Never promise income, earnings or guaranteed results for agents.
- Never describe plan benefits, costs or availability. Never compare carriers or plans. Never imply government endorsement or affiliation.
- Describe the affiliation only as "an Integrity company".
- Use real Google review quotes only with written permission. Never invent attributions. Never pair stock faces with quotes.
- Never present stock or AI-generated images as Kellogg events, staff or attendees.
- End every deliverable with "Pending compliance review" and a list of open items.

## Content Examples

### Excellent Example (reminder email)
**Subject:** Your session is tomorrow at [TIME] [TIME ZONE]

Hi [FIRST NAME],

[EVENT TITLE] starts tomorrow, [DATE], at [TIME] [TIME ZONE]. Here is your link: [JOIN LINK].

You will leave with [ONE-SENTENCE OUTCOME]. If you cannot make it live, we will send the replay.

Questions? Call Kellogg at (801) 816-0505.

See you there,
[NAME], Kellogg Insurance Marketing, an Integrity company

*Why it works: date, time and time zone come first, the outcome is specific, there is no hype, and every unconfirmed fact is a placeholder.*

### Example to Avoid
"Don't miss our AMAZING exclusive webinar! Top agents are earning more with the best carrier plans. Seats are filling fast!!!"

*Why it fails: hype, an earnings implication, an implied carrier comparison, false urgency, and exclamation marks. Fix: state the topic, date, time and outcome plainly.*
